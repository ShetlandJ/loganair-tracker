Loganair cut its highest fares and raised its lowest

On 17 November Loganair announced lower fares for islanders booking at short notice. Its own fare calendar, read automatically since 10 October, shows that cut. It also shows something the announcement left out: the cheapest fare went up on every Sumburgh route at the same moment.

Read before quoting. Every figure is the cheapest one-way fare Loganair's website calendar showed for the whole day. It isn't the price of a particular flight, it hasn't been checked against the final checkout total, and it can't see Loganair's dearer Fly Flex and Fly Flex Plus tickets. Nothing here shows Loganair's statement to be wrong. The "Safe to say" section below gives wording that stays within what the data supports.

What Loganair announced

As reported by Shetland News on 17 November, Loganair said that:

  • it had made more lower-priced fares available on Shetland services for customers booking at short notice;
  • on Orkney and Shetland services to and from Edinburgh and Glasgow, the most expensive fare had been reduced by more than a fifth;
  • the new arrangements were effective immediately.

The chief executive, Luke Farajallah, was quoted as saying: "If you can plan your travel in advance, you'll still get our lowest fares."

The report doesn't mention Aberdeen, and doesn't mention any change to the lowest fares.

Finding 1 · Strong

The cheapest fares went up on every route

The same check that first showed the top fares falling also showed the lowest fare on each route going up. Each route's lowest fare had been the same on every day from 20 October to 3 November.

The chart shows the whole Sumburgh to Edinburgh calendar: the same 284 flight dates, counted by the fare they carried just before the change and two days after. The dearest fares go and so does the cheapest. Afterwards, 200 of the 284 dates sit between £169.99 and £199.99.

Sumburgh to Edinburgh is shown because the rise in the cheapest fare is clearest there. The table below covers all six routes.

Not one of the dates that carried the old lowest fare is at or below it today. From Sumburgh to Edinburgh, £149.99 was the fare on 153 of the 311 dates on sale. The 147 of those still on sale now cost £169.99 to £263.99, and typically £179.99.

Put the two ends together and the change roughly cancels out. Across all 1,730 flight dates we could compare, on every route, 957 got dearer and 720 got cheaper, and the average change was a fall of £1. Broadly, flights in the next 60 days on Edinburgh and Glasgow got cheaper and flights further ahead got dearer. The table under Finding 3 gives the split for each route.

How this was measured, and its limits
  • "Lowest fare" is the lowest price shown on any date up to 330 days ahead. "Before" is the check at 11:31 on 4 November. "Now" is the check at 13:28 on 25 November.
  • We don't know whether the old lowest fares were standard or promotional prices, or how long they had been in place. Far-ahead dates have only been tracked since 19 October. It's a question for Loganair.
  • The chart counts the 284 dates that had a fare at both checks, so it shows 144 dates at £149.99, not 153. Three dates still showed £149.99 two days after the change. All had moved by 10 November. From Aberdeen to Sumburgh, the old £97.99 stayed on one date until mid-November.
  • Not every far-ahead fare rose. Of 1,447 flight dates more than 60 days ahead, 861 got dearer, 542 got cheaper and 44 were unchanged. Sumburgh to Glasgow went the other way: 126 cheaper, 97 dearer.
  • ADS fares can't be measured the same way, because Loganair's ADS calendar shows no price for most far-ahead dates. Where it does, the cheapest resident fare from Sumburgh to Edinburgh is now mostly £94.51 or £99.51, against £84.51 before. A few dates still show the old price: for 24 April 2027 the calendar shows a full fare of £179.99 and an ADS fare of £84.51 (checked on the live site on 25 November). We can't tell whether that price can still be booked or the ADS calendar hasn't caught up.

Finding 2 · Strong

Short-notice fares fell on Edinburgh and Glasgow

Before 4 November, the highest fare the calendar showed on the Edinburgh and Glasgow routes was £384.99 to £388.99 one way. Since then it has been £299.99, which is 22% to 23% lower. On the Aberdeen routes it went from about £354 to £289.99, 18% lower.

The highest fare matters most close to departure, once the cheaper fares have gone. The chart shows the cheapest fare on sale two days before each flight, for flights before the change and flights since.

Looking at the same flight dates on either side of the change gives the same answer. Of 185 Edinburgh and Glasgow flight dates in the following 60 days, 158 were cheaper two days after the change than just before it, 9 were unchanged and 18 were dearer. The typical saving was £25 to £30 one way.

Island residents' fares fell with them. After the ADS discount, booking two days ahead on Edinburgh and Glasgow typically cost £192 to £215 before the change and £160 to £175 since.

How this was measured, and its limits
  • "Two days before" is the fare at the last check on or before the end of the day two days before the flight. "Before" covers flights from 21 October to 5 November. "Since" covers flights from 9 to 27 November. Flights on 6 to 8 November are left out because the new fares were still coming through when they were two days away.
  • "Typical" is the median. The before and since columns are different flights in different weeks, so some of the gap could be down to the time of year. The same-date comparison doesn't have that problem, but it only spans two days.
  • £299.99 is the highest fare normally shown, not a guaranteed ceiling. A fare of £369.99 was the cheapest on sale from Sumburgh to Edinburgh for 22 November, the day before that flight. On 16 and 17 November, £369.99 also appeared on around 50 Glasgow flight dates between January and March, and £359.99 on three Aberdeen dates, before going back to lower prices.
  • "No fare shown" means the calendar showed no price that day. That can mean sold out, not on sale or no flight, and the data can't tell which. Those dates are left out of the typical price.

Finding 3 · Mixed picture

On Aberdeen, most fares went up slightly

The highest fare fell on the Aberdeen routes too, but few Aberdeen flights were ever priced at it. Before the change, the typical fare two days ahead was £259 from Sumburgh and £298 to Sumburgh, against a top fare of about £354. Since the change it has been £290 from Sumburgh and £275 to Sumburgh.

On the same flight dates, either side of the change, 78 of 98 Aberdeen dates in the following 60 days got dearer and 20 got cheaper. The typical change was a rise of £5 to £7 one way.

The table compares the same flight dates for every route: the fare just before the change and the fare two days later.

How this was measured, and its limits
  • "Before" is the check at 11:31 on 4 November. "After" is the check at 23:29 on 6 November, two days later, because the new fares took about two days to come through on every date.
  • A date counts only if it had a fare at both checks. Flights on 4 to 6 November are left out.
  • Two days of ordinary movement are included. In ordinary four-day stretches, 100 to 271 of about 1,700 dates rise and almost none fall, so ordinary movement can't explain a change on this scale.
  • "Typical change" is the median of each date's own change, including the dates that fell.
  • The £1 average fall across all 1,730 dates is an average over flight dates. It isn't weighted by how many people travel on each date or what they paid, so it says nothing about Loganair's income.

Finding 4 · Fact

The new fares appeared on 4 November

A typical check records about 16 fare changes. The check at 11:31 on 4 November recorded 33. The next one, at 18:51, recorded 1,540, and new prices kept arriving for two more days. The fall in the highest fare, which matches Loganair's "more than a fifth", and the rise in the lowest fare both date from that check.

Nothing similar shows up around the announcement. Between 16 and 19 November, 1,551 of 1,738 flight dates (89%) kept the same fare, 158 rose and 29 fell. Five other stretches of the same length show 100 to 271 rises each, so that is ordinary movement.

The data only sees the cheapest fare of the day. If the prices of Fly Flex or Fly Flex Plus tickets changed on 17 November, it wouldn't show here.

How this was measured, and its limits
  • A "fare change" is a flight date whose cheapest fare differed from the previous check, including a fare disappearing or coming back. Times are GMT.
  • The change arrived somewhere between the checks at 11:31 and 18:51 on 4 November. The data can't narrow it further.
  • On 16 and 17 November the calendar was unsettled: fares disappeared from many dates and came back, and the £369.99 fare described under Finding 2 appeared briefly. By 19 November almost all of those dates were back at their earlier fare.
  • Loganair may have had reasons for the gap between the fares changing and the announcement. The data shows when the calendar changed and nothing more.

Safe to say, and what to avoid

Supported by the data

  • "When Loganair cut its highest fares to Shetland, it raised its cheapest ones at the same time: by between £3 and £22 on all six Sumburgh routes we track."
  • "Loganair's highest fare between Shetland and Edinburgh or Glasgow fell from about £386 to £300 one way, as the airline said."
  • "The £149.99 fare from Sumburgh to Edinburgh was on sale on 153 dates before the change. It is on none now. The cheapest is £169.99."
  • "On the Aberdeen routes, most flights in the following 60 days got slightly dearer, not cheaper."
  • "The new fares were already on sale on 4 November, 13 days before the announcement."
  • "These are Loganair's own published fares, checked automatically about every five and a half hours."

Not supported

  • "The cut isn't real" or "Loganair misled people."The top fares fell as stated, and short-notice fares on Edinburgh and Glasgow are lower.
  • "Fares went up overall."More dates rose than fell, but the average change was close to zero, and Sumburgh to Glasgow mostly fell.
  • "Loganair made the cut pay for itself."That needs the number of seats sold at each price, which only Loganair has.
  • "Loganair broke its word on advance fares."The statement was that the lowest fares are still found by booking ahead. That is true. They are just higher than before.
  • "The £149.99 fare was the normal price."We only have it from October. It may have been promotional.
  • "Aberdeen was left out of the cut."Its highest fare fell 18%. It's the typical Aberdeen fare that didn't fall.
  • "Residents' cheapest ADS fares have gone up."Likely, since the discount is taken off the fare, but the ADS calendar still shows some old prices.
  • Any claim about Fly Flex or Fly Flex Plus tickets, or about a specific flight time.The data is the cheapest fare across the whole day.

Questions only Loganair or Transport Scotland can answer

  • Were the lowest fares raised as part of the same review, and why wasn't that part of the announcement?
  • Were the old lowest fares, such as £149.99 from Sumburgh to Edinburgh, standard prices or promotional ones?
  • Why did the typical short-notice fare on Aberdeen not fall, when it did on Edinburgh and Glasgow?
  • How many seats on a typical Shetland flight are sold at the lowest fare and at the highest, and what does Loganair expect the change to do to the average fare paid?
  • Is £299.99 now the highest standard fare on Edinburgh and Glasgow, given that £369.99 has been shown since?
  • On what date did the new fares take effect?
  • For Transport Scotland: what is the expected effect on ADS spending, and have residents' lowest ADS fares changed?

What the data can't show

  • What people actually pay. We see the cheapest seat on sale each day, not how many seats sell at each price or what the average passenger pays.
  • Dearer ticket types. Fly Flex and Fly Flex Plus prices aren't in the calendar.
  • Whether it lasts. The new fares have been in place for three weeks, all in a quiet part of the year. Christmas and summer will be the test.
  • The longer history. Tracking began on 10 October, so we can't say what fares were before then.

How the data was collected

  • Source. The fare calendar on loganair.co.uk: the date picker that shows the lowest fare for each day when you search for a flight. The same request the website makes is sent automatically, once for full fares and once with the Air Discount Scheme option selected.
  • What's recorded. For every date up to 330 days ahead, the lowest one-way fare per person shown for that day. A new row is written only when a date's fare changes.
  • Coverage. Edinburgh and Aberdeen full fares from 10 October 2026. Glasgow, all ADS fares and dates more than 120 days ahead from 19 October 2026.
  • Frequency. 191 checks between 10 October and 25 November. The usual gap was about 5½ hours and the longest was 12 hours.
  • Not checked. Whether the calendar price matches the final checkout total including any fees.
  • Earlier note. The 8 November briefing covers Sunday fares and the rise in a flight's final fortnight. Its short-notice prices were mostly measured before this change.
  • Open data. The code and full history are public at github.com/shetlandj/loganair-tracker, so anyone can check the figures.
