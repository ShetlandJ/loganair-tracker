The two questions asked

1

When were the before and after fares captured?

  • Before: the check at 11:31 GMT on 4 November 2026.
  • After: the check at 23:29 on 6 November, for the comparison of the same flight dates either side of the change. The new fares took about two days to reach every date, so the after check is two days later.
  • Now: the check at 13:28 on 25 November, for the lowest fare currently on sale.

These are three of 191 checks. Every check is in the files below, so any other pair can be compared.

2

Which fare products were compared?

The lowest one-way fare per person that the fare calendar on loganair.co.uk shows for each day. We take this to be the Fly fare, as the lowest-priced product. The fall we measured in the highest fare on the Edinburgh and Glasgow routes, 22% to 23%, matches Loganair's figure for maximum Fly fares. We can't see Fly Flex or Fly Flex Plus prices. Corrections are welcome if the calendar shows anything else.

How the fares were collected

  • Source. The fare calendar (date picker) on loganair.co.uk. A script sends the same request the website makes when the calendar opens, once for full fares and once with the Air Discount Scheme option selected.
  • What's recorded. For each of the six routes, the lowest one-way fare shown for every date up to 330 days ahead. A row is written each time a date's fare differs from the last one recorded.
  • Coverage. Edinburgh and Aberdeen full fares from 10 October 2026. Glasgow and all ADS fares from 19 October.
  • Frequency. Scheduled hourly. In practice the scheduler ran about every 5½ hours: 191 checks between 10 October and 25 November, with at least one on every day. The longest gap was 12 hours.
  • Open. The code and full history are public at github.com/ShetlandJ/loganair-tracker.

Finding 2

The lowest fares rose on all six routes

Each route's lowest fare was the same in every check from 20 October to 4 November. It changed in the same update as the highest fare. None of the dates that carried the earlier lowest fare, and are still on sale, is at or below it on 25 November.

How many dates carry the lowest fare differs by route. On the Aberdeen routes the new lowest fare is on about twice as many dates as the old one was (105 against 57, and 118 against 47). On the Edinburgh and Glasgow routes it is on fewer.

Finding 4

The change arrived in one update on 4 November

A typical check records 16 changed full fares. In the 119 checks before 4 November, the most in any one check was 97. The check at 18:51 on 4 November recorded 1,540. New fares kept arriving over the next two days (476, 224 and 178 changes in the following checks). The change happened between the checks at 11:31 and 18:51; the data can't narrow it further.

The tracker picked the change up automatically at that 18:51 check, and the data was committed to the public repository three minutes later, at 18:54 on 4 November (commit 548ffe0). GitHub timestamps every commit, so the record of what the calendar showed and when was made at the time, not put together afterwards.

Source: checks.csv. Dates seen for the first time, such as new dates entering the 330-day window, are counted separately and not included in these figures.

Limits

  • Each figure is the lowest fare shown for the whole day, not the price of a particular flight.
  • Calendar prices have not been checked against the final checkout total.
  • A day with no fare shown can mean sold out, not on sale, or no flight. The data can't tell which.
  • Checks ran about every 5½ hours, so a fare that changed and changed back between checks wouldn't be seen.
  • Tracking began on 10 October, and far-ahead dates on all routes only from 19 October. The earlier lowest fares were seen unchanged for about two weeks before 4 November. We don't know whether they were standard or promotional, how long they had been in place, or whether they would have changed anyway.
  • ADS fares are included in the files, but the ADS calendar shows no fare for many dates far ahead, so the findings above use full fares only.
  • Nothing here measures what passengers paid or how many seats were sold at each price.

Questions we'd welcome answers to

  1. Were the lowest Fly fares on the Sumburgh routes changed on 4 November, as part of the same update as the reduction in the maximum fares? If so, why?
  2. Were the earlier lowest fares, such as £149.99 from Sumburgh to Edinburgh, standard or promotional prices? Are fares at those levels expected to be offered again?
  3. Does the fare calendar on loganair.co.uk show the Fly fare, and does it match the price at checkout?
